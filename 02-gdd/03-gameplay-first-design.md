# UNDERSTUDY — Gameplay-First Design (v3)

> **This supersedes `00-lore-loop-charter.md` and `02-investigation-psyop-sim-charter.md` wherever they conflict.** Lore is an *idea bank* for now (see `01-lore-bible/`); nothing here depends on it. Where it helps, the lore's names are used as placeholders ("the Cause").
> **Rule:** the game must be fully playable, and fun, with the lore stripped to "something swaps your mind into a different townsperson every morning."

## 1. The fantasy, one paragraph

You wake in a stranger's body in a small town. Nobody notices. Every morning it's a different person. Your real body is somewhere in town, and something is *doing this to you*. You are a detective with no badge and no continuity: your only evidence comes from the lives you borrow. Find out how and why. Find your body. Stop it.

**The players split by themselves, and the game never judges either:**
- **The sandbox player** discovers that every life is a toy box. They work, love, rob, reform, humiliate and rescue. The town simulates them faithfully, and it's *great*. Nothing nags them. They hit Day 15 having lived a dozen lives and wrecked or healed a town, and they get a **complete, satisfying "Long Day" ending**. They never learn there was a case.
- **The detective** notices the town is *too regular*: the swaps follow a rule. They work out the rule, predict tomorrow's body, **steer it**, use each life as an access key to a different part of the case, find their body, and beat the Cause.

The game's job is to make the second path *discoverable by curiosity alone* and *completely optional*.

## 1b. Day 1: your own body, your own voice

**The player starts as a character they create** (name, pronouns, how they talk, what they say when stalling) and plays a normal, short day in their own body. They are led to believe this is the game: you are this person. Then, at 4:12 a.m., they wake up as someone else.

- **Your voice never changes.** In every body, the lines you choose to say as yourself come out in the voice you made. Narration about "you" always uses your name and pronouns.
- **Two ways to answer anyone while you're wearing them:** `[As <host>]` keeps your cover (free of suspicion now, but it wears your sense of self down: **Drift**), or `[You]` speaks as yourself (it holds your sense of self but costs a raised eyebrow in proportion to how unlike the host you sound). Dull players drift into being the host. Sharp players spend cover deliberately.
- **Day 1 is a tutorial in your own body:** a real breakfast, neighbours, a Notebook and a camera, a Decision you make that evening, and a very quiet night.

**Foreshadowing on Day 1: present, never named.** Nine fair-play beats, each innocent on its own, guarded by a test that no give-away word ever appears:

| Beat | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| A big decision | You make one tonight. So did everyone you will become: each host's home holds a trace of theirs |
| "Visiting" | People who decide big things are then "visiting". The diner counts three since July |
| The clock | The Town Hall clock stops at 4:12. Only people who can't sleep hear it |
| Restless people | A deputy writes down who is about to do something big, "usually a week before" |
| The weeks before Founders' Day | "A lot of folks rush in the last weeks of summer... slowly" |
| Same time tomorrow | A phrase on every calendar, nobody says goodbye |
| The Notebook | Your own handwriting: WRITE IT DOWN. You always think you'll remember. (It matters later) |
| Pets | Your stray cat is perfectly normal. Later, every host's pet reacts to you like a stranger |
| The ribbon | At 3 a.m. there is a red ribbon on your door that wasn't there at dusk. Later hosts' doors have one too |

A player who puts these together by Day 5 has solved the Rule: the swapper takes whoever has just decided to change their life, and it marks them first.

## 2. The five verbs

| Verb | What it is | Why it's fun |
|---|---|---|
| **LIVE** | Run a day as this person: work, eat, flirt, steal, confess, quit | The Sims half. Total freedom, real consequences |
| **LOOK** | Search their home, phone, receipts, diary. Read the room | The scene-of-crime half. Tactile, timed |
| **LISTEN** | Talk to people who think you're someone else. Catch contradictions, press with evidence | Interrogation. Your cover is a weapon and a liability |
| **LINK** | Case Board: timeline, suspects, motive/means/opportunity, theories | Deduction. Fair-play, provable |
| **STEER** | Change *who you wake up as tomorrow* by changing the town tonight | The new verb. The investigation becomes active |

## 3. STEER: the missing verb

**The Cause needs a host every morning.** It picks the person who is *most ready to change*: highest **restlessness** (from the sim), plus a hidden pull we call **priority** (the authored baseline, so that with no intervention the story runs in its authored order).

- Restlessness is a sim need. It rises with stuck routines and the Thin Weeks, and falls with company, rest, fun, a good day. It is *observable*: people who are on the edge stare at the lake, miss work, quarrel.
- The detective can **calm** a person (company, a gift, a kind word, a night visit) or **unsettle** one (a scare, a rumour, bad news).
- A swing of about 30 points flips the order between adjacent candidates. One nudge does nothing. **Two deliberate nudges steer.** The baseline is robust to the sim's normal noise (tested across seeds).
- **Somebody always pays.** Calm tonight's host and someone else takes it. You choose *who*. Sometimes that's the person whose life you need next: the mayor's vault, the radio station, the dental chair. Sometimes it's a person you like.

This is both the detective game ("predict the next one, then control it") and the Sims game ("what do I do to this person's evening to change their night?").

## 4. How the case works (fair play, no lore needed)

The case has three layers. **Each layer has a different solution path, and each can be reached by more than one route.**

1. **The Rule** — *why are people being swapped?* Evidence in the lives: the hosts share something. The solution is a *fair-play inference*: list the nine hosts so far, tabulate restlessness, last visitor, last meal, last place, and the rule falls out. The Board has a **Pattern** grid for exactly this. Proving the rule means predicting tomorrow's host correctly **twice** (predict, check at dawn).
2. **Your body** — *where is it?* A physical location with an access chain: who holds the keys, who feeds it, who signs the roster. The clues are in different people's lives and each lives a day. The Board assembles an **Access chain** and the final step is a **Visit** on a day when you're in a body that can open the door.
3. **The Cause** — *what is it and how do you stop it?* A confrontation staged on a day you choose, at a place you choose, with a person you've steered into position. It is a *puzzle that uses the sim*, not a boss fight.

Sandbox players hit none of these gates. Nothing blocks them.

## 5. Psychological pressure (without cheating the player)

The player is never lied to by the *interface*. They're lied to by **people and channels who have a reason to**:

| Source | Truthful? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| NPCs under questioning | Often not | Self-protection, grief, loyalty. Contradictable |
| The Gazette | Selective | It is the town's PR. It's soft on quarrels and theft, silent on swaps |
| Rumours | Mixed | Seeded by factions; spread and distort in the sim |
| Planted evidence | Sometimes | Left by someone to steer you. Every card shows **provenance** (who left it, when, who gains) |
| The calm voice (the Prompter) | Mixed | Hints that are 80% true and 20% steering. It is *not* the UI: it is a voice, labelled as a voice |
| **The Hush (tampering)** | Hostile | Your own case file can be altered overnight if you leave it unguarded. Photograph evidence into the Notebook. **Counterplay is taught in the first three days.** |

**Fair-play rule:** every deception has a tell the player can find, and every punishment has a taught counter.

## 6. The sandbox must be genuinely good

Because this is half the product. The Sims side needs:

- **Needs, mood, routines, relationships, money, jobs, memories, rumours** for everyone (28 now; ~120 later).
- **A reactivity layer**: what you did in a body is *remembered* by the town and brought up later, in any body. Theft is discovered, a confession is gossiped, a promotion is congratulated, a rescue is repaid.
- **Long arcs** for the lives you left: you see them again (Night visits, Encore days, an end-of-run Reckoning).
- **No guilt-trip UI.** Ruin a life, and the game shows you what happened in the Gazette (the soft version) and the ledger (the real one). Fix a life, and the same. It never says what you "should" do.
- Irrevocable **Decisions** (quit, sell, leave, propose, confess) carry a permanent cost to the person who carries on, and the game says so *before* you commit.

**Incentive without a leash:** playing a person the way the clues say they would is rewarded with **Access**: doors that open because the town recognises you as "still you". Deviating is rewarded with **Stories**: emergent events nobody wrote. Detectives lean toward Access. Sandbox players lean toward Stories. Both are real rewards.

## 7. The clock

Fifteen days. The town has a deadline (a public event on Day 15) and your body is **failing** (a visible clue, not a timer: its condition gets worse each day you haven't found it). Sandbox players can ignore the clock entirely; it shows up as a clue, never a nag.

## 8. Endings

| Ending | How |
|---|---|
| **The Long Day** | You never solved it. You lived. It's a good ending, and it's complete |
| **Home** | You found your body and escaped, but the Cause is still there |
| **Case Closed** | You found your body *and* stopped the Cause, with everyone you steered alive or accounted for |
| **Cost** | Case closed, but someone paid for it, and the game shows who. Your steering decides |
| **Settled** | You chose to stay in a life you loved after solving it. Allowed, and it's earned |

## 9. Why this holds together

- One cause, one rule, one body: a **solvable** mystery that a smart player can *win*.
- The sim is not decoration: it **is the evidence** and it is **the lever** (steering). Dull players enjoy it as a toy. Smart players read it as a crime scene.
- Deception is **in-fiction and fair**, and the punishments teach.
- Nothing forces the investigation, so the dull path is a real game, not a trap.

## 10. What gets built, in order

1. **The Cause and STEER** (selection rule, night actions, observable tells, host-by-life flow), tested across seeds.
2. **The reactivity layer**: conversations that read the sim.
3. **The case file**: Pattern grid, timeline, access chain, theory.
4. **Night visits and the Reckoning**: seeing the long arc of lives you touched.
5. **Body search**: the access chain and the Visit.
6. **The confrontation puzzle.**
7. Content breadth (Days 6–15), ambient population, audio, portraits, walkable town.
