# UNDERSTUDY — The Lore–Loop Charter

> **Rule of the whole project: the lore is the product, and the gameplay is the reward for understanding it.**
> Every minigame is a *proof* of something true about Little Mercy. Every truth you prove gives you a new toy. Players who don't care about the lore still get a complete, juicy, cozy game. Players who do care get a game that quietly gets bigger every time they understand something.

This charter supersedes any conflict with `01-lore-bible/`. The canon (who, what, why) is unchanged; what changes is how it is *delivered and operated*. A change list is at the end.

---

## 1. Two audiences, one game

| Player | What they want | What the game gives them | Where they stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cozy** | Charm, pets, mini-games, "one more day" | Complete 15-day story with a satisfying ending (**Staying Day**), gorgeous diorama, 13 distinct toys, collectibles | Credits. Happy. |
| **Engaged** | A mystery to solve | The Case Board, tells, Seals that unlock powers, endings 1 and 2 | After ending 1/2, sees "something's missing" and starts a Season 2 run |
| **Obsessive** | A world that survives scrutiny | 40 Seals, 90 fragments, 30 cross-references, the Community Roll, the secret fourth ending, a canon that answers fan theories | Never. This is the audience that makes the game last. |

**Design test for every feature:** *can a player enjoy it without understanding it, and enjoy it more if they do?* If it fails either half, redesign it.

**The dull-mind guarantee:** nothing in the game is gated behind reading. Every Seal can be earned through *doing* (play the vignette, find the object, hear the sound). Reading adds colour; it never blocks progress. Wren (the hint voice) offers a plain-language nudge after two failed attempts.

**The deep-mind guarantee:** nothing in the game is explained to the player. Seals are *proved by the player*, not announced by the game, and several can be proved in more than one way.

## 2. The three public questions

A game must have a question that sells it in one breath. Ours has three, and the first is answered by the end of Act I so the player trusts the game to pay off.

| Q | When asked | When answered | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Who is dressing my bedroom?** (red ribbon, kettle on, pet fed) | Day 1, minute 2 | Day 5 (the Hymn) / Day 14 (Nell) | Trust in the game |
| **2. What am I?** | Day 1 (hands, pets bow) | Day 14 | The twist |
| **3. What happened in 1961, and what does the lake want?** | Day 3 onward | Day 15 | The ending |

**The pitch, one line:** *Every morning someone in town vanishes. Every morning you wake up as them. Nobody can find out.*

## 3. The loop: Wake · Perform · Step Off · Night · Slack

One in-game day = **12–15 minutes** of play, designed for a commute, a lunch break or a Switch couch session.

| Beat | Time | What you do | Lore it operates | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WAKE** | 2–3 min | Timed bedroom search; the pet reacts; learn who you are from objects, not a briefing | The Hush (you must infer your own life) | Props (clues) |
| **PERFORM** | 5–6 min | Morning conversations, then the job vignette | The role as a lens | Notes, Applause, a *Proof Tool* |
| **STEP OFF** | 1 min | At dusk you leave the life. A card shows the Notes you've left in the Remnant | Remnants (R13) | The "it sticks" moment |
| **NIGHT** | 4–5 min | One of five locations: search, eavesdrop, pin evidence, run a Seal test | The Slack, the Case Board | Evidence → Seals |
| **THE SLACK** | 30 sec | 4:12. The Take, as a short, beautiful, slightly horrifying scene. Tomorrow's face is revealed last | R5, R8 | The hook for the next day |

Seen from a distance the loop is Animal-Crossing cozy with a Her Story case board under it.

## 4. Roles are lenses: the Proof Tool principle

**You only have each role for one day, and each role has one tool that can prove a certain kind of truth.** This is the economy of clues:

> *Evidence* is found by anyone. *A Seal* can only be completed with the right Proof Tool, which only exists on the right day, in the right life.

So the player carries evidence across days and waits for the day they hold the right lens. That is the whole reason the roles exist, and it makes "it sticks" and "bleed and overlap" serve the clue economy:

- **Overlap as tool access.** Your earlier Remnants will run their old tool for you if they like you (WARMTH) or fear you (NERVE). Mags's Remnant will run the dental overlay on Silas on Day 9, if you were kind to her on Day 1.
- **Bleed as discovery.** A habit from a past role can unlock a Seal in a later one (the drummer's tap opens the Tape; the child's "why?" breaks the Mayor's deflection).
- **Replay.** A Season 2 run makes you want to do things differently in a different order. The Seals you missed are the reason.

## 7. The Model: progress as a thing you can look at

In the Prop Room there is a diorama of Low Mercy under glass: the drowned town as a tabletop model. **Every Seal places one piece in it**: a roof, a lamp-post, the church spire, a tiny dentist's chair. It is:

- The **progress UI** (40 pieces, no menus)
- The **collection hook** for cozy players (miniatures, pets, props; shareable screenshots)
- The **visual spoiler-proof memory** of what you understand
- In Ending 4, the **lamp** lights it, one window at a time, 212 of them.

|---|---|

A casual player's ending is a *good* ending, not a failure. The three others are the ones that make people talk.

## 9. Lore beyond the game

The audience that stays is built outside the app as much as inside it:

1. **WMRC 88.1 live stream.** A real, in-world radio station that plays the Dedication Hour. The songs spell FAIR WATER over a month.
2. **Little Mercy Public Library site.** An in-world web page with the Index, the Pageant programs and Priya's unfinished entries.
3. **The Community Roll.** Every player's pinned evidence contributes one fragment of one of 212 names; the Roll fills in for everyone and unlocks S39 for all.
4. **Season mode ("Same Time Tomorrow").** Opt-in: one in-game day unlocks per real day at 4:12 p.m. local; a global conversation runs for 15 days. Binge play stays available.
5. **Shareable Theory Cards.** A one-screen summary of a player's board, with no spoilers.

---

## 10. Changes to the lore bible

None of these overturn canon. They deepen it so the lore is *operable*.

1. **Roles became lenses** (§4): each Life's job vignette is now the proof mechanism for a branch of the truth.
2. **Seals** replace the Seven Keys as the unit of progress; the Seven Keys remain as the Ω subset (S37–S40 + K1–K7).
3. **The Model** replaces a log or journal as the progress UI.
4. **Day 14 and Day 15 are gameplay** (the Prop Room and the Raising), not cutscene.
5. **The Community Roll** is a canonical object: the bell-tower register can be completed by players.
6. **Ending 3 is the warm default.** It is a good ending, no longer the "stay" failure case.
7. **Nudge** lets the player shape a Decision's form, giving agency inside a fixed 13-day spine.
8. **The Inner Voice (Wren)** is also the accessibility voice: she gives plain-language hints after two failed attempts.
