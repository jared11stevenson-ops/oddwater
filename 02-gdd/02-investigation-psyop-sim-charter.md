# UNDERSTUDY — The Investigation, the Psyop, and the Sim

> Supersedes anything in earlier docs that conflicts. Spoilers. The canon underneath (the Wish, the Held, the Chorus, the Hush) is unchanged. What changes is **what the player believes they are doing**, and **how much of the town is alive when they aren't looking.**

## 1. The player's story (what they are told)

*You wake in a stranger's body with a stranger's life. Nobody notices. You remember who you are: an investigator from outside, sent to look into the people who go missing in Little Mercy. Your real body is somewhere in town. Every morning you wake up in someone else. Find out why. Find your body. Stop it.*

That is the **Briefing**. It's the first thing the game says. It has a case file, a cause, a villain and a finish line, and every part of it is built to feel like a homicide investigation.

## 2. The joke (what is true)

**The Briefing is a role.** The Chorus has no outside life, no investigator's body, no case. It woke on 26 August with a *script in its head*, written in the Playhouse Prompt Book on page 0: **"The Investigator."** A part, with a motive and a costume (a trench coat on the Prop Room rail, sized for no one). Nell wrote it, in her own hand, the night before the Chorus woke, because a Swing with a purpose stays on stage and a Swing with no purpose does what Wren wanted: *refuses.*

The "own body" is the bait. The lake, the Holdfast and the Keeper all want the same thing from the Chorus, which is for it to **settle**: to find a body, take it, stay, and stop making change.

- **A smart player** notices the Briefing is *too tidy*: it names no outsider organisation, no case number, no colleague, no date they last saw their own face. They stop trusting the goal and start asking who benefits from the player having one.
- **A dull player** follows it, finds Room 9 at Lakeview Rest (a comatose stranger in the player's "face"), takes the body, and settles. That is Ending 3 or 1, depending on the town's state, and it is a *warm, happy-looking ending*. The joke is on them. It's what the town built the Briefing to produce.

## 3. Who is lying to you (the propaganda ecosystem)

Four streams feed the player information all the time. **None of them is entirely false.** Each is a *voice with an interest.*

| Stream | Voice | What it wants you to believe | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Prompter** | A calm, helpful voice in your head ("Write that down."). Sounds like Wren at first. It isn't always Wren | You are an outsider; your body is the goal; the Gardener (Bea) is the killer | Hints that are 80% true and 20% steering. Planted clues. The red ribbons |
| **The Holdfast** (Dolores, Hank, Mayor's office) | Official channels: bulletins, council minutes, the radio news, the school curriculum | The vanishings are *relocations*; the Waking are fanatics; nothing happened in 1961 | Paper that is always there and always tidy; clues that point at the Waking |
| **The Waking** (Silas) | Dedication codes, clockwork hints, careful whispers | The lake is a prison; wake everyone; play the Hymn | Genuine evidence, but cut to recruit. Withholds what makes the Waking look reckless |
| **The Gardener** (Bea) | Warmth. Welcome loaves. "Life's short, go for it!" | The Taken are safe, and the Held deserve lives; join the Chorus to them | Kindness. Nudges. Never lies. Omits |

The **Hush** is the fifth, and the biggest: it is the lake's own editing of what the town remembers and what *you* remember (see §5).

Every clue carries **provenance**: who left it, when, and what they gain. The Case Board shows it. Ignoring provenance is how you accuse the wrong person.

## 4. It plays like a homicide investigation

Each vanishing is a **case file**. The player does what detectives do:

| Detective work | In the game |
|---|---|
| **Scene** | The victim's home is the scene. You *live in it*. Their habits are the victimology |
| **Timeline** | A minute-by-minute reconstruction of the victim's last 24 hours, built from statements, receipts and phone records. Gaps are leads |
| **Means, motive, opportunity** | Every person of interest has a grid. The Board will not let you name a suspect with an empty cell |
| **Interrogation** | NPCs make **claims** in conversation. Later, in a different body, you can **present evidence** that contradicts a claim. Presenting the right card cracks it. The wrong card burns trust |
| **Forensics** | Proof Tools (dental overlay, the Reply, the vault, the Reel...) turn a theory into a **Seal** |
| **Witness handling** | People remember you in *the last body*. Your reputation walks around town without you |
| **Chain of custody** | Evidence can be tampered with. See §5 |
| **The theory** | Name a culprit and a mechanism on the Board before the Raising. The ending is decided by how right, and how kind, you were |

## 5. The Hush attacks the investigator

The cruellest rule: **the case file is not safe.** At dawn, the Hush edits evidence cards. Quietly:

- "Fair water" becomes "Fair weather". "212" becomes "112". "Rook" becomes "Brook". Nothing flashes. Nothing announces.
- An edited card **fails its Seal test** with no explanation. The strings "don't hold."
- **Counter:** photograph a card at the Board (limited film each day). Photos live in the **Notebook**, which the Hush can't reach. A card that disagrees with its photo shows a small red mark. You can then restore it from the photo, or by re-visiting the source.
- Edits grow with **Drift** (§6) and with the Hush's strength (a calm town). A frightened town (fog) is *harder* to edit. Airing the Hymn is dangerous, and also the best defence.

A player who never checks their own board will think they are going mad. They are being gaslit by a lake, correctly.

## 6. Drift: comfort as the trap

Living a life well is **pleasant**. That is by design, and it is the horror. When you play in character, meet the person's needs, do their job, eat their food and be kind to their family, you earn **Contentment**: warm lighting, a lighter score, happy small events, collectibles and little rewards. This is the Sims side of the game and it's *genuinely fun*.

It also raises **Drift**, a hidden number, shown to the player as a friendly label ("Settled in"). Drift is the Hush winning:

| Drift | What changes |
|---|---|
| low | You are a detective in someone's shoes. Tools are sharp |
| medium | The Prompter speaks more. Cards get edited more. Tell Sight flickers |
| high | You stop opening the Case Board without noticing. The Briefing starts to feel like a dream. The game *offers you Room 9* |
| max | You accept the body. **Ending: the Settling.** A cozy, tragic ending. Staying in one life, forever |

The player is never told the number. A careful player sees the signs and **chooses to stay un-comfortable on purpose**: eat badly, sleep late, break the routine, ask the wrong question at the wrong time. Being *bad* at living the life is how you stay awake.

## 7. Freedom: you can wreck or mend a life

You control a person for a day. **Nothing is forbidden.** You can:

- Empty their savings. Quit their job. Burn the diary. Propose. Confess. Sleep with their rival. Gossip. Gift. Sabotage. Sell the house. Say the unsayable. Tell a child the truth.
- Do none of it and live a lovely, ordinary day.

Because the town **keeps simulating after you leave**, it all sticks (see §8). The rule that makes it dangerous and meaningful is the lake's own:

> **A Remnant is a walk-on. A walk-on who makes a Decision is Struck.**

*Ordinary* changes (money, mood, a fight, a rumour, a promotion, a wrecked friendship) persist in the person who carries on. A **Decision** (selling the house, quitting for good, leaving, marrying, confessing to a crime, a life-changing, irrevocable act) is a Decision in the lake's sense: that Remnant is **Struck** at the next 4:12 and their place is empty. You can mend a life, or you can end it, and the town can see which. The game tracks both as *evidence in your favour or against you*.

**Incentive to play the clues.** Playing a person as the clues say they *would have* played (their habits, their Decision, their secret) earns **Alignment**: it unlocks the case's hidden evidence, because the town's lie is shaped to fit the person you are pretending to be. Deviating costs access, and buys **Notoriety**: emergent stories, new rumours, strange visitors, and a very different town. Both are fun. They don't give the same ending.

## 8. The Sim (heavy, Sims-like, always running)

Every named person in Little Mercy (29 today, ~120 later) is a simulated **person** with a daily life whether or not you are watching.

- **Needs:** Energy, Hunger, Social, Fun, Comfort, **Purpose**, and **Restlessness**.
  *Restlessness* rises when a life is stuck and when the Thin Weeks are on. When it peaks, a person is on the edge of a Decision. It's a *measurable predictor of vanishings*, and the Decision Radar ability simply lets you read it.
- **Mood** from needs. Mood changes what they say and do.
- **Schedule and autonomy:** each person has a routine (home, work, haunts), and chooses what to do from needs, schedule and personality (utility AI). They move through the town, eat, work, drink, worship, gossip, quarrel.
- **Relationships:** affinity and trust between every pair who've met. Co-location creates events.
- **Money and work:** wages, savings, rent, theft, gifts, jobs lost and won.
- **Memory:** people remember what they saw you do, in *whatever body*.
- **The rumour network:** information moves through the town as **rumours** with a subject, a claim, an origin, a truth value and a belief level per person. Rumours spread through conversation, distort in the retelling, and are **seeded on purpose** by factions (propaganda). The radio is a mass channel. A rumour you start can become the town's truth, or ruin someone.
- **Town Report:** every dusk, a short report on what happened while you weren't looking, in the voice of the local paper (which is a propaganda organ, and may lie).

## 9. How a day works now

1. **Wake.** Snoop the life. Learn who they are. The Prompter says something helpful.
2. **Live.** Play the day as them: free action, work, conversations, vignettes. The sim runs around you.
3. **Press.** Use claims and evidence you carry from *other* bodies. This is the interrogation loop.
4. **Dusk.** You step off. The Remnant carries on, and **the sim runs the rest of the day and night**, producing the Town Report.
5. **Night.** Investigate as nobody. Photograph evidence. Build the case. Check the timeline.
6. **The Slack.** 4:12. Someone else vanishes. Overnight the Hush edits your board.

## 10. Endings (revised)

| Ending | How you get there |
|---|---|
| **The Settling** | You accept the body (Room 9), or Drift maxes. Warm, quiet, wrong |
| **Same Time Tomorrow** | You believed the Holdfast's story and helped finish the pact |
| **Low Water** | You believed the Waking and opened the gates, whatever it cost |
| **Staying Day** | You played every life well and never asked why. Everyone is fine. Nothing changes |
| **Fair Water** | You triangulated all four streams, protected your evidence, proved the Seals, did right by the people you wore, and gave the town the goodbye it couldn't say |
| **The Culprit** | New. You named a *person* instead of a mechanism. If you were wrong, someone paid for it, and the game shows you who |

## 11. What the build does with this (priority)

1. Sim kernel: needs, schedules, autonomy, relationships, money, memory, rumours, restlessness, Decision/Strike, Town Report.
2. Claims and the presenting-evidence interrogation.
3. Evidence integrity: Hush edits, photographs, the Notebook.
4. Drift, Contentment, the Prompter's lures, Alignment and Notoriety.
5. Case file structure: timelines and means/motive/opportunity grids.
6. The Briefing, and the Room 9 trap and its reveal.
